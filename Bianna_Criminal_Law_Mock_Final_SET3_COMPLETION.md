**I. Eleanor’s Liability (Continued)**

Under the *proximate cause* test, Leo is liable because he set in motion a chain of events that he intended to cause death. The fact that Dr. Alistair made a choice does not break the chain if that choice was a foreseeable consequence of the threat. **Best argument:** Leo is the proximate cause of Richard’s death. ### PART IV: ESSAY 2.5 — COMPARISON

**Comparison of Attempt Law: Common Law vs. Model Penal Code (MPC)**

The liability of Eleanor for the attempted murder of Leo, and the liability of Leo for the attempted murder of Richard (via threat), requires a comparison of the two major approaches to attempt law.

**1. The Act Requirement (Actus Reus)**

- **Common Law:** The common law requires a *dangerous proximity to success* or a *substantial step* that is *unequivocal* (the "last act" test or the "physical proximity" test). The act must be so close to the completed crime that it is almost certain to succeed unless interrupted. A mere threat or preparation is not enough.
    - *Application to Eleanor:* Eleanor’s act of stabbing Leo is clearly a substantial step. It is the last act needed to kill him. She is liable under the common law.
    - *Application to Leo:* Leo’s act of threatening Dr. Alistair is not a "last act." He did not perform the final act necessary to kill Richard (that was Dr. Alistair’s omission). Under the common law, a threat alone is likely *preparation*, not attempt. He would not be liable for attempted murder under the common law.

- **Model Penal Code (MPC):** The MPC requires a *substantial step* that is *strongly corroborative of the actor’s criminal purpose* (MPC § 5.01). This is a broader test. It allows for attempt liability even if the actor has not yet performed the last act. The focus is on whether the conduct confirms the actor’s intent.
    - *Application to Eleanor:* Eleanor’s stabbing is a substantial step. She is liable under the MPC.
    - *Application to Leo:* Leo’s threat, combined with his intent to cause Richard’s death, is a substantial step that strongly corroborates his purpose. He is liable for attempted murder under the MPC. The threat is not just preparation; it is the key act that sets the plan in motion.

**2. The Intent Requirement (Mens Rea)**

- **Common Law:** The common law requires *specific intent* to commit the target offense. Even if the underlying crime is a general intent crime (like murder), attempt requires a specific intent to cause the result.
    - *Application to Eleanor:* Eleanor specifically intended to kill Leo. She satisfies the specific intent requirement.
    - *Application to Leo:* Leo specifically intended to cause Richard’s death. He satisfies the specific intent requirement.

- **Model Penal Code (MPC):** The MPC requires that the actor act *with the purpose of causing the result* (MPC § 5.01(1)(b)). This is essentially the same as specific intent. For result elements, the actor must have the *conscious object* to cause the result.
    - *Application to Eleanor:* She acted with purpose. Satisfied.
    - *Application to Leo:* He acted with purpose. Satisfied.

**3. Impossibility**

- **Common Law:** The common law distinguishes between *factual impossibility* (a defense) and *legal impossibility* (not a defense). Factual impossibility is when the actor’s goal is illegal, but circumstances prevent its completion (e.g., picking an empty pocket). Legal impossibility is when the actor’s goal is not a crime (e.g., receiving stolen goods that are not actually stolen). Modern common law has largely abolished factual impossibility as a defense.
    - *Application to Eleanor:* No impossibility issue. She could have killed Leo.
    - *Application to Leo:* No impossibility issue. Richard was a living person. The plan could have succeeded.

- **Model Penal Code (MPC):** The MPC explicitly abolishes impossibility as a defense if the actor’s conduct is otherwise criminal (MPC § 5.01(1)(a)). The focus is on the actor’s intent, not the actual possibility of success.
    - *Application to Eleanor:* No issue.
    - *Application to Leo:* No issue.

**4. Abandonment (Renunciation)**

- **Common Law:** The common law generally does not allow abandonment as a defense to attempt once a substantial step has been taken. The crime is complete at the moment of the act.
    - *Application to Eleanor:* She did not abandon.
    - *Application to Leo:* He did not abandon.

- **Model Penal Code (MPC):** The MPC allows an *affirmative defense* of renunciation if the actor abandons his effort *voluntarily* and *completely* (MPC § 5.01(4)). The abandonment must not be motivated by a fear of detection or increased difficulty.
    - *Application to Eleanor:* She did not abandon.
    - *Application to Leo:* He did not abandon.

**Conclusion of Comparison:**

- **Eleanor:** Liable for attempted murder under both the common law and the MPC. Her act was a substantial step, and she had the specific intent to kill.
- **Leo:** Liable for attempted murder **only under the MPC**. Under the common law, his threat would likely be considered mere preparation, not a substantial step. The MPC’s broader "substantial step" test allows for liability based on conduct that strongly corroborates intent, even if the actor has not yet performed the last act. This comparison highlights the MPC’s more expansive approach to inchoate crimes, focusing on the actor’s dangerousness rather than the proximity to completion.

**Final Summary of All Parties:**

- **Eleanor:** Guilty of Murder of Richard, Attempted Murder of Leo. No defenses.
- **Leo:** Guilty of Murder of Richard (under proximate cause theory). Not guilty of attempted suicide. Duress and necessity fail. Under common law, not guilty of attempted murder of Richard (preparation only). Under MPC, guilty of attempted murder of Richard.
- **Dr. Alistair:** Not guilty of any homicide. His use of force against Leo was justified self-defense/defense of others. No criminal liability.
