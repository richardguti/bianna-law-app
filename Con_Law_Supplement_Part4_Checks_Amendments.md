# SECTION A: THE MASTER "CHECKS & BALANCES" MATRIX

*This matrix lays out every formal check each branch can wield against the others. Use it when an exam question asks about a conflict between branches, or when you need to argue whether a branch has exceeded its power.*

| **Branch Being Checked** | **Check Exercised By** | **Mechanism** | **Constitutional Source** |
|--------------------------|------------------------|---------------|----------------------------|
| **Congress** | President | Veto (regular & pocket); threat of veto influences legislation. | Art. I, §7 |
| | | Calling special sessions of Congress. | Art. II, §3 |
| | Judiciary | Judicial review — declaring acts of Congress unconstitutional. | *Marbury v. Madison* (1803) |
| **President** | Congress | Impeachment (House impeaches; Senate tries & convicts by 2/3 vote). | Art. I, §§2-3; Art. II, §4 |
| | | Override presidential veto (2/3 both houses). | Art. I, §7 |
| | | Senate confirmation of appointments (simple majority). | Art. II, §2 |
| | | Senate ratification of treaties (2/3). | Art. II, §2 |
| | | "Power of the purse" — appropriation of funds; can defund executive actions. | Art. I, §9 |
| | | Congressional oversight & subpoenas. | Implied from Art. I |
| | Judiciary | Judicial review — declaring executive actions unconstitutional or illegal. | *Youngstown*, *Marbury* |
| | | Criminal prosecution of a former president (no immunity for unofficial acts). | *Trump v. United States* (2024) |
| **Judiciary** | Congress | Control over the size and budget of the Supreme Court (court‑packing). | Art. I, §8; Art. III, §1 |
| | | Power to make "exceptions and regulations" to Supreme Court appellate jurisdiction. | Art. III, §2 cl.2; *Ex parte McCardle* |
| | | Senate confirmation of federal judges. | Art. II, §2 |
| | | Impeachment of judges. | Art. I, §§2-3 |
| | President | Appointment of federal judges (with Senate confirmation). | Art. II, §2 |
| | | Pardon power — can pardon federal criminal contempt. | Art. II, §2 |
| **States** (collective) | Federal Government | Article V amendment process — states must ratify amendments (3/4 of states). | Art. V |
| | | Senate representation (each state gets two senators). | Art. I, §3 |
| | | Electoral College — states choose electors. | Art. II, §1 |
| **Federal Government** | States | Supremacy Clause — federal law preempts conflicting state law. | Art. VI, cl.2 |
| | | Federal courts can review state court decisions involving federal questions. | *Martin v. Hunter's Lessee*; Art. III |

> **Fisher Exam Tip:** If you see a question that asks "Can Congress / the President do X?" immediately check this matrix to see if the other branch has a tool to stop it. Fisher loves questions that force you to argue *both* the action's validity and the available checks.

### 🏛️ Fisher Trap: The "Structural Checkmate" Pattern

When a branch overreaches, the exam answer should:
1. **Identify the overreach** (cite the relevant Article/case)
2. **Identify the check** the other branch could deploy
3. **Argue both sides** — is this really an overreach, or is it within the "zone of twilight"?

```
Example: President issues an executive order regulating emissions.
→ STEP 1: Is there congressional authorization? (Youngstown Zone analysis)
→ STEP 2: If Zone 3, Congress's check = legislation overriding the order + override veto
→ STEP 3: Judiciary's check = judicial review (standing + merits)
```

### 🏛️ Fisher Alert: The "Incorporation Trick"

Not all Bill of Rights protections apply against the states. The exam trap:

| **Incorporated** (Apply to States via 14th Amend. Due Process) | **NOT Incorporated** |
|---|---|
| 1st (speech, religion, press, assembly, petition) | 3rd (quartering — not formally decided) |
| 2nd (arms — *McDonald*) | 5th Grand Jury indictment requirement |
| 4th (searches & seizures) | 7th (civil jury trial) |
| 5th (self-incrimination, double jeopardy, due process, takings) | |
| 6th (all rights) | |
| 8th (cruel & unusual, excessive fines — *Timbs*) | |

> **Fisher Trap:** If a fact pattern involves a STATE (not federal) government denying someone a grand jury hearing, the answer is: **No constitutional violation.** The 5th Amendment Grand Jury Clause is one of the few Bill of Rights provisions NOT incorporated against the states.

# SECTION C: CRIMINAL PROCEDURE PRIMER (AMENDMENTS 4–8)

*The V2 outline assumes Fisher's course focuses on structural constitution, due process, and equal protection. But if your exam includes a search, confession, or criminal trial fact pattern, deploy these frameworks.*

## 4th Amendment — Searches & Seizures

**Core Rule:** Government needs a warrant based on probable cause to search, UNLESS an exception applies.

**Key Exceptions:**
| Exception | Rule | Key Case |
|---|---|---|
| Consent | Voluntary consent = no warrant needed | *Schneckloth v. Bustamonte* |
| Search Incident to Arrest | May search person + area within immediate control | *Chimel v. California* |
| Plain View | Officer lawfully present sees contraband in plain view | *Arizona v. Hicks* |
| Automobile | Cars get reduced expectation of privacy; need PC but no warrant | *Carroll v. United States* |
| Exigent Circumstances | Emergency (destruction of evidence, hot pursuit, danger) | *Kentucky v. King* |
| Stop & Frisk | Reasonable suspicion → brief stop + pat-down for weapons | *Terry v. Ohio* |
| Special Needs | Drug testing, school searches, border searches | *Vernonia*, *T.L.O.* |

**Exclusionary Rule (*Mapp v. Ohio*):** Evidence obtained in violation of 4th Amend. is inadmissible ("fruit of the poisonous tree"). Exception: Good faith reliance on warrant (*Leon*).

---

## 5th Amendment — Self-Incrimination & Miranda

**Miranda v. Arizona (1966):**
Before custodial interrogation, officers must warn:
1. Right to remain silent
2. Anything said can be used against you
3. Right to an attorney
4. If you can't afford one, one will be appointed

**Triggers:** Both (1) **custody** AND (2) **interrogation** must be present. Public encounters or voluntary conversations don't require Miranda.

---

## 6th Amendment — Right to Counsel

- **Attaches** at the initiation of formal adversarial proceedings (indictment, arraignment).
- *Gideon v. Wainwright*: Right to appointed counsel for indigent defendants in ALL felony cases.
- *Strickland v. Washington*: Ineffective assistance = (1) deficient performance + (2) prejudice (reasonable probability result would have been different).

---

## 8th Amendment — Cruel & Unusual Punishment

**"Evolving Standards of Decency" Test (*Trop v. Dulles*):**
- Death penalty: Constitutional for murder (*Gregg*), unconstitutional for rape of adult (*Coker*), unconstitutional for non-homicide crimes (*Kennedy v. Louisiana*)
- Juveniles: No death penalty (*Roper*); no mandatory LWOP (*Miller v. Alabama*)
- *Timbs v. Indiana* (2019): Excessive Fines Clause incorporated against states

# SECTION D: THE 10th AND 11th AMENDMENTS — DEEPER DIVE

## I. The Anti-Commandeering Doctrine (10th Amendment)

**The Rule:** Congress cannot **commandeer** state governments — it cannot force state legislatures to pass laws or force state executive officials to enforce federal programs.

| Case | What Congress Tried | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| *New York v. United States* (1992) | Forced states to either regulate radioactive waste or "take title" to it | INVALID — commandeers state legislatures |
| *Printz v. United States* (1997) | Required local sheriffs to conduct background checks under the Brady Act | INVALID — commandeers state executive officials |
| *Murphy v. NCAA* (2018) | Federal law prohibited states from authorizing sports betting | INVALID — commandeers by telling states what laws they *cannot* pass |

> **Fisher Distinction:** Congress CAN:
> - Attach conditions to **federal funding** (Spending Power — *Dole*)
> - **Preempt** state law entirely (Supremacy Clause)
> - Regulate **individuals** directly (Commerce Clause)
>
> Congress CANNOT:
> - Order state officials to implement federal programs (*Printz*)
> - Order state legislatures to legislate (*New York*)
> - Prohibit states from changing their own laws (*Murphy*)

---

## II. State Sovereign Immunity (11th Amendment)

**The Rule:** States cannot be sued in federal court by their own citizens or citizens of other states, unless:

1. The state **consents** to the suit;
2. Congress **abrogates** sovereign immunity using a valid power source; OR
3. The suit is against a **state officer** for prospective injunctive relief (*Ex parte Young* fiction).

**The Abrogation Question:**

| Power Source | Can Congress Abrogate? | Key Case |
|---|---|---|
| Commerce Clause | **NO** | *Seminole Tribe v. Florida* (1996) |
| Art. I powers generally | **NO** | *Seminole Tribe* |
| § 5 of the 14th Amendment | **YES** — if congruent & proportional to documented violations | *Fitzpatrick v. Bitzer* (1976) |
| § 2 of the 13th Amendment | **YES** (probably) | Not yet definitively resolved |

> **Fisher Connection:** This links directly to *Boerne*. Congress must demonstrate a *pattern* of unconstitutional state behavior to abrogate immunity under § 5. A prophylactic law that sweeps too broadly fails *Boerne*'s "congruence and proportionality" test.
